# Workshop Strategic Structure

Guide/template for structuring the workshop (webinar) that feeds the funnel, from script to offer. Fill in each section before recording — this becomes the final recording script.

## 1. Full structure, from opening to pitch

An effective conversion workshop follows an emotional progression, not just an informational one. Recommended structure (60-90 min):

| Block | Duration | Objective | What goes in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Opening + promise** | 3-5 min | Grab attention in the first 60s, reaffirm why the person signed up | Strong hook (question, statistic, 10-second story), reaffirmation of the promise made in the ad/signup page, "what you'll walk away knowing today" (3 bullets) |
| **2. Quick credibility** | 2-3 min | Answer "why listen to you" without sounding like a résumé | Your origin story condensed into 1 turning point (the moment you discovered the method), light social proof (numbers, not compliments) |
| **3. The real problem** | 5-8 min | Get the audience to recognize the pain more clearly than they had before | Name the problem in a way no one has named it before, debunk 1-2 "false solutions" everyone tries (and why they fail) |
| **4. The turn (new mechanism)** | 5-8 min | Introduce why the method works when everything else doesn't | The name of your method/framework, the core idea in 1 sentence, why it solves the root cause and not the symptom |
| **5. Value content (the real "workshop")** | 25-40 min | Actually teach — 3 to 5 steps of the framework | Each step: what → why → how (real example/case) → common mistake. Leave 1 step deliberately incomplete ("step 4 is where most people get stuck on their own — that's exactly what we solve inside [offer]") |
| **6. Transition to the pitch** | 2-3 min | Move from "lesson mode" to "offer mode" without breaking the pace | Recap what was taught, name the gap that remains (the "how to do this fast/without mistakes/on your own"), ask if they want to see how to speed it up |
| **7. Offer presentation** | 10-15 min | See section 4 (Offer Construction) | — |
| **8. Close + scarcity** | 5-8 min | Convert now, not later | Recap the offer in 30s, reinforce real scarcity/urgency, final objections, final CTA repeated 2-3x |

**Golden rule**: the content in block 5 needs to be good enough to stand on its own (someone who never buys should still walk away satisfied) — that's what generates word-of-mouth referrals and reduces refunds from those who do buy.

## 2. Recording direction

- **Format**: split screen (you + slides) or you on closed camera with full-screen slides interspersed — avoid slide-only the entire time, the audience needs to see your face during connection moments (opening, story, pitch).
- **Speaking pace**: slower and more deliberate in blocks 1, 4, and 7 (key ideas need to "sink in"); faster and more practical in block 5 (execution).
- **Audio**: priority #1 — dedicated microphone, no echo. Bad audio kills retention more than bad video.
- **Slides**: 1 idea per slide, large text, no paragraphs. If there's a sentence worth memorizing, it is the entire slide.
- **Simulated interaction**: insert lines like "several people are asking..." / "every time I teach this someone asks..." — creates the feeling of a live event even when recorded (essential for the evergreen format).
- **Cuts**: record in separate blocks (by section of the table above), making it easier to re-record just the pitch if the offer changes without re-recording everything.
- **Actual vs. perceived duration**: 60-75 min is the sweet spot — long enough to feel valuable, short enough to finish in one sitting.

## 3. Retention, authority, and conversion strategy

**Retention** (keeping the person watching until the pitch):
- Promise something specific for the "end" right in the opening ("at the end I'm going to show you exactly [X]") — this creates an open loop that only closes if they stay.
- Reinforce the loop every 15-20 min ("hold on to this, because it'll make sense at the end").
- Never let a block die without linking to the next ("and that brings me to the real problem behind why most people get stuck here...").
- If the format is evergreen with simulated chat, schedule timed chat messages at the points of highest drop-off risk (first 5 minutes, and right before the pitch).

**Authority** (why trust you):
- Proof by results > proof by diploma. Concrete numbers (even small ones) beat a title.
- Name authority objections before the audience thinks of them ("I know there are a lot of gurus selling this, the difference here is...").
- Show the method working in real time whenever possible (screen share of a real case), not just talk about it.

**Conversion**:
- The buying decision is made emotionally and justified rationally — block 3 (problem) and block 7 (offer) carry the emotional weight; block 5 (content) carries the rational justification ("this person really knows what they're talking about").
- Never hide the price until the end — hint at it early ("later on I'll show you how to get this for a lot less than you think") to reduce anxiety and mid-session drop-off.

## 4. Building the offer and call to action

**Offer structure** (value stack):
1. **Main product/program** — what solves the central transformation promised in the workshop.
2. **2-4 bonuses**, each solving a specific objection or accelerator (e.g., "bonus for those short on time," "bonus for those who've already tried and failed before"). Each bonus needs its own name and a stated perceived value.
3. **Guarantee** — reduces perceived risk to zero; the stronger it is (e.g., unconditional for N days), the more it converts, as long as the product actually delivers.
4. **Real scarcity** (not fake): limited spots due to support/cohort capacity, or a promotional price valid only for those watching right now/until a real deadline. Invented scarcity is detectable and burns the brand.

**Pitch order**:
1. Recap the transformation (not the product — the transformation).
2. Present the main program and how it delivers that transformation step by step.
3. Stack the bonuses one by one, with stated perceived value for each.
4. Show the total stacked value vs. the actual price (the "value bridge").
5. Present the guarantee.
6. Present the scarcity/urgency.
7. Clear, repeated CTA: what to click, what happens next (e.g., "click the button below, you'll go to a simple payment page, and as soon as you confirm I'll unlock the first module").

**CTA**: use a single action verb and repeat it exactly the same way every time (e.g., always "secure your spot," never vary the phrase) — this reduces cognitive load at the moment of decision.
